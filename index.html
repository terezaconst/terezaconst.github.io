Abstract

I am a postdoctoral fellow in the Biogeochemistry Unit of the Department of Environmental Science at Stockholm University. I model methane emissions from subsea permafrost on the Arctic Ocean shelves under present and future conditions as part of the SuPerTip project.

Introduction

I grew up in Nicosia, Cyprus, and moved to the UK after high school to study Physical Natural Sciences (BA/MA) and Astrophysics (MSc) at Trinity College, University of Cambridge. I then took two years out before my PhD, working remotely as an ML lead at a startup and travelling wherever COVID allowed (see the non-research section below).

I completed my PhD at the Institute of Astronomy, University of Cambridge. My thesis, Venus as a baseline in the search for life, reframed a central challenge in astrobiology: to find life, we must first define lifelessness. Life may be recognisable as a deviation from abiotic expectations: a potentially biological anomaly. Venus is Earth's nearest planetary twin and is uninhabitable today, making it a natural control case. I studied Venus's atmosphere, volcanism, and weathering to understand the planetary processes that shaped its environment. This work culminated in a framework for searching for life—and its absence—beyond our Solar System.
